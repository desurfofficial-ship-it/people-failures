Thomas Edison

1,000+ failed attempts at a practical light bulb

Edison treated every unsuccessful experiment as data. When asked about the failures he replied that he had simply found thousands of ways that would not work. The eventual carbon-filament bulb changed daily life.

c. 1879

Walt Disney

Fired for “lacking imagination”; early studio bankruptcies

A newspaper editor told young Walt he lacked imagination. Laugh-O-Gram went bankrupt. He later built an entertainment empire defined by imagination.

Ousted from Apple, the company he co-founded

In 1985 Jobs was forced out. He returned in 1997 and led the company’s greatest resurgence — iMac, iPod, iPhone — reshaping multiple industries.

Colonel Harland Sanders

Recipe rejected more than 1,000 times

Sanders pitched his fried-chicken recipe to restaurant after restaurant well into his 60s. The 1,009th (or so) “no” was followed by a yes that created KFC.

Albert Einstein

Labeled slow; struggled early academically

Did not speak fluently until around age nine and was considered a weak student by some teachers. Later transformed physics with special and general relativity.

Abraham Lincoln

Multiple election losses and business failures

Failed in business, lost races for legislature, Congress and Senate, and endured profound personal losses before becoming one of America’s most consequential presidents.

King discarded the draft of Carrie. His wife retrieved it. The novel launched one of the most commercially successful writing careers in modern history.

Near-bankruptcy of both Tesla and SpaceX in 2008

In late 2008 both companies were days from collapse. Musk committed remaining personal capital. The companies later redefined electric vehicles and private spaceflight.

Early ventures collapsed. The third attempt introduced the Model T and the moving assembly line, transforming manufacturing and personal mobility.

5,126 failed prototypes over 15 years

Dyson spent years and his savings iterating bagless vacuum designs. Prototype 5,127 worked. Manufacturers refused; he built the company himself.

Rejected multiple times by USC film school

Denied entry to the University of Southern California’s film program. He later directed Jaws, the first modern summer blockbuster, and became one of cinema’s defining figures.

Rejected by Toyota; factory destroyed in WWII

Early piston-ring work was rejected. A factory was bombed and later leveled by an earthquake. Honda rebuilt and created one of the world’s great automotive companies.

Record for most strikeouts in an era of home-run glory

Ruth held the career strikeout record for decades while also rewriting the home-run record books. Failure at the plate was inseparable from his dominance.
